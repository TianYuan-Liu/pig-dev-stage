Molecular evidence for porcine developmental stages via a calibrated transcriptomic classifier
Introduction

The domestic pig (Sus scrofa) has become an indispensable biomedical and agricultural model, owing to its remarkable anatomical, physiological, and genomic similarity to humans. Compared with rodents, the porcine genome is functionally closer to humans, supporting superior translational relevance for drug discovery, disease modeling, and xenotransplantation. Pigs also share key features of human biology—including gyrencephalic brain architecture, cardiovascular physiology, and comparable organ sizes—facilitating direct translation of surgical interventions and medical device testing. Beyond biomedical applications, accurate developmental staging is equally critical for agriculture, where the timing of breeding and nutritional interventions directly impacts productivity and economic returns.

Current porcine developmental staging frameworks are largely morphological, spanning Carnegie-adapted embryonic stages through postnatal categories such as neonatal, nursery, growing/finishing, and adulthood. However, these systems are constrained by subjectivity, inter-observer variability, and the requirement for invasive or destructive sampling. Even under standardized criteria, embryologists frequently disagree on stage assignment, and archived samples often lack morphological integrity while retaining high-quality RNA. Chronological age is also an imperfect proxy: littermates often develop asynchronously, and continuous molecular transitions are compressed into discrete categories. These limitations hinder studies requiring precise synchronization, such as transcriptomic time courses or environmental intervention experiments, where subtle staging errors obscure biological signals.

Molecular approaches offer a transformative alternative. Recent advances in transcriptomic “clocks” have demonstrated near-theoretical accuracy for predicting biological rather than chronological age, with validations across C. elegans, mouse, and human datasets. In pigs, large-scale resources such as PigGTEx and single-cell atlases now provide unparalleled molecular resolution across tissues and developmental windows. Unlike morphology, transcriptomes capture both discrete cell-type emergence and continuous developmental trajectories, enabling pseudotime inference and detection of sub-stages invisible to visual inspection. Moreover, RNA-based clocks exhibit far greater dynamic range than epigenetic clocks, enhancing sensitivity to environmental, nutritional, and pathological influences that modulate developmental pace.

Concurrently, machine learning advances now permit calibrated, high-dimensional classification of developmental states. Elastic Net regression and gradient-boosted models have proven highly effective for transcriptomic data, while probabilistic calibration methods (e.g., temperature scaling, isotonic regression) translate predictions into reliable confidence estimates. Such calibration is critical for biological interpretability, allowing researchers to distinguish confidently staged samples from uncertain cases. Integrating these computational advances with comprehensive transcriptomic resources creates the opportunity for a robust, scalable, and objective framework for porcine developmental staging.

Here we present a calibrated transcriptomic classifier that systematically infers porcine developmental stage across tissues, overcoming the technical and conceptual limitations of morphology-based systems. By integrating cross-tissue transcriptomic atlases with state-of-the-art machine learning calibration, we establish a molecular framework that is quantitative, reproducible, and applicable to both contemporary and archived samples. This approach not only refines fundamental understanding of mammalian developmental biology but also enables practical applications in precision livestock farming and biomedical translation, setting the stage for molecularly defined developmental benchmarks in the pig model.
