\section{Results}

\subsection{Study design and data landscape}

The visual analytics suite distils the experimental design and data resources into a multi-panel methodology overview (Fig.~\ref{fig:methodology}). Age was harmonised into five ordered developmental stages spanning 0~d to adulthood, and samples were profiled across eight tissues with heterogeneous class balance. Adaptive granularity selected 4-class schemes for muscle, brain, and liver; 3-class schemes for blood, small intestine, and lung; and dichotomous schemes for adipose and testis. The machine learning pipeline includes log-transformed normalisation, variance filtering, mutual information feature selection capped at 2{,}000 genes, ordinal or logistic regression, and bootstrap calibration.

\subsection{Classifier performance across tissues}

Model diagnostics demonstrate consistently high performance on tissue-specific hold-out sets (Fig.~\ref{fig:performance}, Table~\ref{tab:performance}). Balanced accuracy ranged from 0.821 in blood to 0.955 in testis, with macro F1 scores spanning 0.762--0.952. Ordinal mean absolute error was below 0.30 stages in every multi-class tissue, reaching 0.081 in liver and 0.095 in muscle. Spearman correlations between predicted and chronological age ranged from 0.69 to 0.97 across tissues with ordered stages. Confusion matrices reveal that when misclassifications occur they are predominantly adjacent stage swaps, yielding near-miss accuracies above 85\% for all tissues. Chronological age correlations (Fig.~\ref{fig:performance}c) further confirm that calibrated probabilities preserve ordinal structure even in tissues with merged stages.

\subsection{Molecular signatures and feature organisation}

The classifier learns tissue-resolved molecular programs that align with developmental trajectories (Fig.~\ref{fig:signatures}). UMAP embeddings illustrate the separation of 2{,}467 samples into tissue-specific manifolds while preserving intra-tissue developmental gradients. Feature overlap analysis shows that most discriminative genes are tissue-specific, with only a minority shared across more than two organs. Marker expression trends highlight canonical regulators such as \textit{PPARG} in adipose, \textit{MBP} in brain, \textit{ALB} in liver, and \textit{MSTN} in muscle, each exhibiting stage-dependent modulation.

\subsection{Functional enrichment and biological validation}

Gene ontology enrichment of stage-informative gene sets underscores strong tissue-specific developmental programs rather than a shared pan-tissue signature (Fig.~\ref{fig:biovalidation}). Adipose predictors were dominated by regulators of adipogenesis, with 19 of the top 50 markers mapping to fat cell differentiation (fold enrichment $\approx 96$, $q = 5.8 \times 10^{-33}$) and complementary lipid-storage pathways. Blood relied on haematopoietic circuitry: enriched terms included haemopoiesis (fold enrichment $\approx 86$, $q = 6.0 \times 10^{-25}$) and erythrocyte differentiation. Brain stage markers clustered around neurodevelopment, with high-magnitude enrichment for synaptic transmission (fold enrichment $\approx 69$, $q = 5.3 \times 10^{-25}$) and neurogenesis. Liver genes pointed to metabolic and hepatic differentiation programs, including glycolytic process and cholesterol homeostasis.

Respiratory tissue exhibited a distinct developmental axis: lung predictors were enriched for lung development (fold enrichment $\approx 34$, $q = 6.8 \times 10^{-24}$) and respiratory system development. Muscle relied on myogenesis, including skeletal muscle fibre development (fold enrichment $\approx 24$, $q = 1.5 \times 10^{-19}$) and muscle contraction. Small intestine markers were skewed toward epithelial maturation and nutrient handling. Testis predictors were uniquely enriched for reproductive pathways---spermatid development (fold enrichment $\approx 21$, $q = 5.6 \times 10^{-17}$) and male gonad development.

\subsection{Conserved biomarkers validate developmental staging}

To validate that our molecular staging captures biologically meaningful developmental programs conserved across species, we examined the expression trajectories of eight canonical biomarkers with well-established roles in human development (Fig.~\ref{fig:biovalidation}c). Each biomarker was selected based on its tissue-specific function and documented developmental regulation in human studies.

In liver, \textit{ALB} (albumin) showed progressive upregulation from infant to adult stages (Spearman $\rho = 0.72$, $p < 10^{-15}$), mirroring the hepatocyte maturation pattern observed during human liver development where ALB serves as the major plasma protein and hepatocyte differentiation marker. Brain-specific \textit{MBP} (myelin basic protein) exhibited a steep increase between pre-pubertal and post-pubertal stages (4.2-fold change, $p < 10^{-20}$), consistent with the postnatal myelination surge documented in human CNS development. The erythroid marker \textit{HBB} ($\beta$-globin) demonstrated the expected transition from fetal to adult hemoglobin programs in blood samples ($\rho = 0.61$, $p < 10^{-10}$), marking the maturation of the erythroid lineage toward the adult program.

Adipose tissue showed strong upregulation of \textit{PPARG}, the master adipogenesis regulator, with a 3.1-fold increase from infant to adult stages ($p < 10^{-12}$), paralleling human adipocyte differentiation patterns where PPARG is required and often sufficient for adipocyte differentiation. In muscle, \textit{MSTN} (myostatin) displayed complex stage-dependent regulation (ANOVA $F = 18.3$, $p < 10^{-8}$), reflecting its dual role in prenatal myogenesis control and postnatal muscle homeostasis as a negative regulator of skeletal muscle growth. Lung development was marked by progressive \textit{SFTPC} (surfactant protein C) expression, increasing 2.8-fold through maturation ($\rho = 0.68$, $p < 10^{-14}$), mirroring alveolar type II cell differentiation crucial for surfactant homeostasis in humans.

The intestinal stem cell marker \textit{LGR5} maintained stable high expression across all stages in small intestine (fold change range: 0.8--1.2), consistent with its role in marking crypt base columnar intestinal stem cells that drive continuous epithelial renewal. Testis-specific \textit{DMRT1} showed stage-dependent expression aligned with gonadal maturation ($\rho = 0.55$, $p < 10^{-9}$), reflecting its conserved role as an essential transcription factor for male gonadal development and maintenance of male cell fate. Overall, the examined biomarkers showed expression patterns qualitatively consistent with their reported roles in human developmental literature, validating our staging framework's biological relevance and supporting the pig as a translational model for human development.
