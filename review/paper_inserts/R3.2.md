\paragraph{One-to-one ortholog definition.}
Pig (\textit{Sus scrofa}, Sscrofa11.1) and human (\textit{Homo sapiens},
GRCh38) Ensembl gene identifiers were resolved to HGNC-style symbols via
\texttt{mygene.info} (Python \texttt{mygene} client). Candidate one-to-one
orthologs were defined as gene symbols appearing exactly once in the pig
annotation and exactly once in the human annotation (``shared-unique-symbol''
filter). Each candidate pair was then verified against Ensembl
Compara\cite{cardoso-moreira_gene_2019} via the public REST homology
endpoint \texttt{/homology/id/sus\_scrofa/\{gene\_id\}} with
\texttt{target\_species=human} and \texttt{type=orthologues}, retaining
only pairs whose reciprocal Compara classification was
\texttt{ortholog\_one2one} (the Compara confidence-1 equivalent). The
resulting validated ortholog table (cached at
\texttt{review/analyses/results/\allowbreak pig\_human\_one\_to\_one\_orthologs.csv})
is used for all cross-species comparisons.

\paragraph{Human Cardoso-Moreira dataset preprocessing.}
We used the per-sample RPKM matrix \texttt{Human.RPKM.txt} from
ArrayExpress accession E-MTAB-6814 (Cardoso-Moreira~\textit{et~al.}
2019\cite{cardoso-moreira_gene_2019}). Sample columns are named
\texttt{<Tissue>.<stage>.<replicate>}; we restricted to brain, liver,
and kidney columns. Prenatal samples (\texttt{*wpc}) were discarded; only
postnatal stages were retained. Cardoso-Moreira postnatal stage labels
were mapped to our five-stage porcine scheme as follows:
\textit{newborn/infant/toddler}~$\rightarrow$~Infant;
\textit{school/youngTeenager}~$\rightarrow$~Pre-pubertal;
\textit{teenager/oldTeenager}~$\rightarrow$~Post-pubertal;
\textit{youngAdult/youngMidAge}~$\rightarrow$~Adult; and
\textit{olderMidAge/senior}~$\rightarrow$~Adult (oldest sub-bin).
Log$_2$ fold-changes (Adult/Infant) were computed in raw RPKM space with a
pseudocount of 0.01, matching the pig TPM pipeline. Per-gene $p$-values
were estimated by Welch's $t$-test on the human RPKM (small $n$) and by
Mann-Whitney $U$ on the pig TPM (large $n$); both were
Benjamini-Hochberg corrected within the orthologue universe. The strict
filter (FDR$<$0.10 and $|\log_2\mathrm{FC}|>0.5$ in both species) is the
same as that used in the original muscle analysis.
