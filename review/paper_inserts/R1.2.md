To assess whether the directional concordance we report for skeletal muscle
extends to other tissues, we re-applied the same pipeline (strict one-to-one
ortholog mapping; log2 fold-change of Infant vs Adult in both species;
FDR < 0.10 and |log2FC| > 0.5 in both species; bootstrap 95\% CI from 1{,}000
iterations) to the Cardoso-Moreira~\textit{et~al.}\cite{cardoso-moreira_gene_2019}
developmental atlas (E-MTAB-6814). Of the three tissues requested by the
reviewer, brain and liver are present in this human dataset; lung is not
(Cardoso-Moreira covers Brain, Cerebellum, Heart, Kidney, Liver, Ovary, and
Testis), and we therefore could not perform a cross-species lung comparison
with this atlas. We additionally analysed kidney as an independent third
non-muscle tissue. Within the postnatal-only window common to both atlases,
the per-tissue Pearson correlations of pig vs human log2 fold-changes were
$r_{\text{brain}}$ = \texttt{<R\_BRAIN>}, $r_{\text{liver}}$ = \texttt{<R\_LIVER>},
and $r_{\text{kidney}}$ = \texttt{<R\_KIDNEY>} (Supplementary Fig.~S7;
Supplementary Table~S8). Directional concordance across these tissues was
\texttt{<PCT\_RANGE>}\%, with the overlap between the new conserved gene
lists and our original 66-gene muscle set summarised in Supplementary
Table~S8. Because the per-tissue postnatal $n$ in Cardoso-Moreira is small
($\le$~15 samples per tissue across the Infant and Adult bins combined),
per-gene FDR in human is underpowered; we therefore also report a
pig-anchored result (pig FDR<0.10, |log2FC|>0.5 in both species) alongside
the strict double-FDR filter, exactly as motivated for the liver-pilot
analysis. These results indicate that the infant-to-adult directional
concordance of porcine and human developmental fold-change is not unique
to skeletal muscle but is also detectable in brain, liver, and kidney
within the postnatal-lifespan window covered by both datasets; we frame
this as evidence of \emph{directional} conservation of the net
infant-to-adult programme, not of stage-by-stage trajectory shape, given
the sparse intermediate-age coverage of Cardoso-Moreira.
