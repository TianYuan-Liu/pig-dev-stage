\textbf{Biological validation via curated marker genes.}
To test whether the chronological-age boundaries used to define the five
developmental stages correspond to genuine biological maturation switches,
we curated a panel of 25 literature-supported postnatal mammalian markers
spanning four transition windows: suckling-to-weaning (~21 d, e.g. AFP,
ALB, IGF1/2, LCT), weaning-to-growth (~60 d, e.g. MYH3, MYH8, MYH2, MSTN,
IGFBP5, MBP, PLP1, SYP), the pubertal switch (~150 d, e.g. INSL3, LHB,
AR, ESR1, GHR), and adult maturation (~365 d, e.g. CDKN2A, LMNA, SIRT1/3,
MKI67). For each marker x tissue pair we computed mean log2(TPM+1) per
stage, ran one-way ANOVA with Tukey--HSD post-hoc, and identified the
adjacent stage pair carrying the largest mean difference. After excluding
combinations lacking adequate per-stage coverage (>= 5 samples per flanking
stage), 30 marker x tissue pairs were evaluable. Twenty of thirty (67\%)
matched the literature-predicted direction of change and ten of thirty
(33\%) had their largest single-step change at the exact predicted
transition; T1 (suckling->weaning) showed the strongest concordance
(4/7 markers, 57\%). The most informative miss was the porcine myosin
heavy-chain switch: MYH3, MYH8 and MYH2 showed correctly directed but
earlier transitions (Infant -> Early childhood) than the assumed weaning
boundary, consistent with the rapid first-month perinatal isoform exchange
documented in pig skeletal muscle. The full panel and source CSV
(\texttt{review/analyses/results/marker\_gene\_transitions.csv}) and the
4-panel trajectory plot (Supplementary Figure S6) are provided alongside
the analysis script.

These observations provide partial, independent biological support for the
five-stage chronological framework -- particularly the perinatal,
peri-weaning and pubertal switches -- while clarifying that the calendar
boundaries are approximations of underlying maturation programmes that
overlap, and that some adjustments (e.g. an earlier myosin-switch midpoint)
might better match the molecular dynamics in pig muscle.
