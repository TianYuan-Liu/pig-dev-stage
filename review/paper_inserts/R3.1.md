## Suggested Discussion paragraph

To assess whether developmental signals could be artefacts of technical batch, we projected each of the five focal tissues (Muscle, Brain, Liver, Lung, Blood) into PCA and UMAP space on the top 5,000 most variable genes and decomposed the variance of PC1–PC5 with respect to Stage and BioProject (Figure S4; Supplementary Table S\ref{tab:batch_variance}). In our dataset, developmental stage explained a substantial share of PC1 variance in four of five tissues (R²_stage = 0.40, 0.65, 0.33, 0.55 for Muscle, Brain, Lung, Blood; n=761, 43, 129, 78 samples drawn from 56, 6, 20, 7 BioProjects respectively), with the UMAPs showing visible Infant → Adult gradients even where multiple BioProjects co-occupy the embedding. BioProject still explains the larger absolute variance share — expected, because most PigGTEx projects sample a narrow age window so the two factors are partially confounded by design — but the developmental signal is reproduced within a single project for muscle (Pearson r = 0.69, n=66 cross-species-validated genes) and is preserved by tree-based models that are robust to monotonic platform shifts.

## Suggested Methods sentence

For each focal tissue, we log2(TPM+1)-transformed the expression matrix, retained the 5,000 highest-variance genes, and computed PCA (n_components = 10) and UMAP (n_neighbors = 15, min_dist = 0.1, metric = "euclidean", random_state = 42). Variance attributable to Stage and BioProject in PC1–PC5 was estimated by ordinary-least-squares R² for `PC ~ Stage`, `PC ~ BioProject`, and the combined model, with one-hot encoded factors; silhouette scores in PC-10 space provided a complementary cluster-quality estimate.
