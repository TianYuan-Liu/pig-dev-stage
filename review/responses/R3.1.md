# Response to Reviewer 3, point R3.1 — Batch Effect Evaluation

We thank the reviewer for this important and well-targeted comment. We agree that within-project muscle validation alone is insufficient to demonstrate that batch effects are not driving developmental signals across all tissues, and we have therefore added a global, multi-tissue batch-effect evaluation.

**New analysis.** We performed PCA (10 components) and UMAP (n_neighbors=15, min_dist=0.1, metric="euclidean", random_state=42) on log2(TPM+1) values of the top 5,000 most variable genes for each of the five focal tissues (Muscle, Brain, Liver, Lung, Blood). Embeddings, metadata, and a per-tissue OLS variance-decomposition table (`PC ~ Stage`, `PC ~ BioProject`, `PC ~ Stage + BioProject`) for PC1–PC5 are saved at `review/analyses/results/batch_effect_variance_decomposition.csv`, with the companion silhouette scores at `review/analyses/results/batch_effect_silhouette.csv`. A new multi-panel UMAP figure (Figure S4) shows side-by-side BioProject and Stage colourings for all five tissues (`paper/figures/output/pdf/figS4_batch_effects.pdf`).

**Key findings.** Developmental stage alone explains a substantial fraction of PC1 variance in four of the five tissues — Muscle R²_stage = 0.40 (n=761 samples / 56 BioProjects), Brain R²_stage = 0.65 (n=43 / 6), Lung R²_stage = 0.33 (n=129 / 20), Blood R²_stage = 0.55 (n=78 / 7); only Liver is dominated by batch on PC1 (R²_stage = 0.02 vs R²_bioproject = 0.94, n=309 / 34 projects), though Liver PC5 recovers a clear stage signal (R²_stage = 0.29). Across all tissues PC1–PC5 the combined Stage + BioProject model captures 50–96% of the variance, with BioProject explaining the larger share — unsurprising given that most PigGTEx BioProjects sample a single narrow age window, so Stage and BioProject are partially confounded by design. Crucially, the UMAPs (Figure S4) show clear Infant → Adult gradients within tissues even when several BioProjects co-occupy the same region of the embedding (most visible in Muscle and Lung). This is consistent with the within-project muscle validation (Pearson r = 0.69 against the full-dataset effect, n=66 cross-species-validated genes) and with the model's held-out balanced accuracy of 0.81–0.96 across all five tissues. We discuss the partial confounding as a stated limitation of the resource rather than evidence of an artefact, and we caveat the claim accordingly in the Discussion.
