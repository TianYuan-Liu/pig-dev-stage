# Response to Reviewer 3, point R3.3

## Reviewer comment
> Biological Validation of Developmental Stages: The developmental stages are
> defined based on physiological milestones. The ML model predicts these
> stages with high accuracy, but this only proves that the stages are
> transcriptionally distinct, not that the boundaries defined by chronological
> age correspond precisely to biological maturation switches. To further
> validate these stage definitions, the authors could analyze the expression
> of known developmental marker genes or pathways at the transition points
> between the defined stages.

## Response

We thank the reviewer for this excellent suggestion. To provide independent
biological support for the staging framework, we curated a panel of 25
literature-supported postnatal mammalian marker genes spanning the four
transition windows of our framework (suckling -> weaning at ~21 d; weaning ->
growth at ~60 d; pubertal switch at ~150 d; adult maturation at ~365 d).
For each marker x tissue combination we computed mean log2(TPM+1) per stage,
ran one-way ANOVA with Tukey-HSD post-hoc, and recorded the adjacent stage
pair with the largest absolute mean difference as the "observed maximum-change
window". Of 25 panel entries x 5 tissues (Muscle, Liver, Blood, Lung, Brain),
30 marker x tissue pairs satisfied our coverage threshold (>=5 samples per
flanking stage). New analysis script and CSV at
`review/analyses/marker_gene_validation.py` /
`review/analyses/results/marker_gene_transitions.csv`, and a new
supplementary Figure S6.

The results provide partial independent support for the staging boundaries
that is informative in both its hits and its misses. Twenty of thirty
evaluable pairs (66.7%) showed the literature-predicted direction of change
across the four windows, including very strong (q < 1e-10) ANOVA signals for
IGF2/IGF1 decline (T1 in muscle), MYH3/MYH8 silencing and MYH2 induction
(T2 in muscle), and AR/GHR induction (T3 in liver). Ten of thirty pairs
(33.3%) had their largest single-step change at exactly the
literature-predicted transition; T1 (suckling -> weaning) had the highest
window-match rate at 4/7 (57%). The most striking misses were the muscle
perinatal myosin switch (MYH3/MYH8/MYH2), where the steepest drop occurs
between Infant (0-20 d) and Early childhood (21-59 d) rather than between
Early childhood and Pre-pubertal (60-149 d) -- a finding consistent with
recent porcine myogenesis literature that places this switch in the first
postnatal month rather than at weaning. We also note that several
endocrine-axis markers (AR, GHR, ESR1, IGFBP5) in muscle peak between
Post-pubertal and the small Adult muscle stratum (n=5), so adjacent-stage
changes for T3 in muscle are downstream of the small Adult subset; we report
these but treat the muscle T4 jump as exploratory.

These findings together indicate that our chronological-age boundaries
capture genuine biological transitions, especially around weaning, the
adult-myosin switch, and the pubertal endocrine activation, but that the
T2 boundary (60 d) sits slightly after the steepest myosin switch in pig
muscle and the post-pubertal -> adult boundary (365 d) is empirically
under-sampled in lung and brain. We add a paragraph to the Discussion and
a new supplementary figure (S6) summarising this analysis, and we explicitly
qualify the staging boundaries in the limitations.
