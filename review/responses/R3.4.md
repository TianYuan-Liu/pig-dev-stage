# Response to Reviewer 3, point R3.4

**Reviewer comment.** *"The paper highlights that developmental programs are
profoundly tissue-specific... Providing the top feature genes for each
tissue-specific model and their biological relevance would significantly
enhance the utility of the atlas..."*

We thank the reviewer for this constructive suggestion; addressing it has
materially improved the interpretability of the atlas. We have generated a
new Supplementary Table S7 listing the top 20 features per tissue ranked by
aggregate LightGBM importance, together with a brief functional description
(mygene.info and UniProt REST; placeholder rows mark genes that returned no
curated description in either resource, mostly novel pig loci and lncRNAs),
the peak developmental stage, and the peak-vs-trough fold change in mean
$\log_{2}$(TPM$+$1). A companion heatmap (Supplementary Figure S5) displays
the row-z-scored trajectories of the top 10 genes per tissue across the five
stages, making the timing of each transition immediately visible. Annotations
and stage means were produced reproducibly by
`review/analyses/tissue_specific_features.py`, which writes a long-format CSV
(`review/analyses/results/tissue_specific_features.csv`) and caches API
responses for offline re-runs.

Several striking, biologically coherent patterns emerge in the four
non-muscle tissues (all values from `tissue_specific_features.csv`):

- **Brain**: the top developmental gene reaching curated annotation is
  *CCDC9B* (rank 2; post-pubertal peak, fold change 1.93), followed by the
  ketogenic enzyme *HMGCS2* (rank 6; infant peak), the Hippo-pathway kinase
  *LATS1* (rank 7; post-pubertal), and the hepatic/CNS transcription factor
  *GLIS3* (rank 8; post-pubertal, fold change 1.33). The shift from
  neonatal ketogenesis to post-pubertal Hippo signalling is consistent with
  established metabolic-to-circuit-maturation transitions in the porcine
  cortex.
- **Liver**: *SATB2* and *CDH7* (ranks 1-2) peak at post-pubertal, while
  *ALDH18A1* (proline/ornithine biosynthesis; rank 3, fold change 3.42) and
  *COL1A1* (rank 6) peak in infancy, mirroring the well-known
  amino-acid-anabolism-to-mature-hepatocyte programme.
- **Lung**: matricellular *CCN5* (rank 1; post-pubertal, fold change 2.71)
  and the neural cell-adhesion molecule *L1CAM* (rank 2) dominate post-
  pubertal alveolar maturation, whereas the oncofetal RNA-binder *IGF2BP2*
  (rank 5; infant peak) signals neonatal mesenchymal growth -- the same
  factor we previously highlighted in muscle, suggesting a shared
  early-postnatal proliferation programme across mesodermal tissues.
- **Blood**: *RPLP1* (rank 1; infant peak, fold change 3.14) and *USF2*
  (rank 4) flag a robust neonatal ribosomal/transcriptional surge, while
  the chemokine receptor *CCR6* (rank 7; post-pubertal) tracks adaptive
  immune maturation.

The new table and figure are referenced from the Results and Discussion
sections (see `review/paper_inserts/R3.4.md` and `review/thesis_inserts/R3.4.md`).
We have intentionally retained the placeholder rows in the table so readers
can see exactly which top-ranked features remain functionally uncharacterised
in the pig genome, identifying clear opportunities for follow-up annotation.
