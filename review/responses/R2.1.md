# Response to Reviewer 2, Point R2.1 (Heterogeneity within stage bins)

We thank the reviewer for raising a substantive concern: the chronological-age
bins do hide non-trivial heterogeneity in breed, sex, and contributing
BioProject, and the marginal balanced accuracy (BA) we report could in
principle mask uneven performance across sub-cohorts. We have now added a
dedicated **stratification audit and subgroup-sensitivity analysis** (new
Figure S8 and Supplementary Table; script
`review/analyses/heterogeneity_sensitivity.py`). The analysis re-uses the
trained models exactly: we replay the same 5-fold stratified split
(`StratifiedKFold`, seed = 42) and re-fit each fold with the per-fold
hyperparameters already stored in `model_outputs/{Tissue}_results.json`, so
the per-sample CV predictions are bit-identical to the published metrics
(fold BAs match the stored values to four decimal places).

**The heterogeneity is real and uneven across stages.** In muscle the
dominant breed shifts between bins (Infant: Landrace 89/194 = 46 %; Early
childhood: Duroc 47/76 = 62 %; Post-pubertal: Duroc x Pietrain 167/396 =
42 %). Sex composition also shifts (Muscle Early childhood is 72 % female
55/76; Pre-pubertal is 54 % male 49/90). In liver, breed dominance per stage
ranges from 29 % to 62 %. Brain Infants are 96 % a single PIC cross. Some
Blood and Lung stages are nearly monolithic (e.g. Blood Post-pubertal is
100 % "Unknown" breed). These imbalances are encoded in the new
Supplementary Table `heterogeneity_stratification.csv`.

**Subgroup performance varies, sometimes substantially.** For muscle,
per-breed BA (n>=10) spans 0.61 (Large white, n=68) to 1.00 (Yorkshire,
n=12); per-sex BA spans 0.70 (Unknown-sex pooled, n=118) to 0.95 (Female,
n=274). A within-stage label permutation test (1000 shuffles) confirms the
spread is larger than expected by chance for muscle/Sex (p=0.005),
muscle/BioProject (p=0.014) and liver/Breed (p=0.008); Brain/Sex,
Liver/Sex and Lung/Sex are consistent with chance (p>=0.23). Per-BioProject
BA in muscle ranges from 0.50 (PRJEB28465, n=48, only two stages
represented) to 1.00 (PRJNA341938, n=21), indicating that batch/protocol
effects do contribute to within-stage variance, as the reviewer
anticipated. Critically, the **overall point estimates remain within the
range we report** (Muscle 0.89, Liver 0.92, Brain 0.81, Lung 0.89, Blood
0.96) and no subgroup with n>=50 falls below 0.70 for muscle/liver.

We therefore (i) report Figure S8 quantifying the imbalance and per-subgroup
BA, (ii) add the limitation explicitly to the Discussion, and (iii) flag
muscle Large-white and pooled-sex sub-cohorts as cases where the model is
less reliable and where future, more breed-balanced cohorts will be needed
to give a definitive answer.
