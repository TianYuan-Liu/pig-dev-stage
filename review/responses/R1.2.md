## R1.2 (Reviewer 1, point 2)

> *"For Result 2.4 Evolutionary conservation with human development. The authors only performed a cross-species analysis using the skeletal muscles between human and pig. However, a previous study (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-019-1338-5) has generated bulk RNA-seq data of brain, liver, and lung in human, macaque, mouse, rat, rabbit, opossum and chicken across their developmental time points from early organogenesis to adulthood."*

**Response.** We thank the reviewer for this important suggestion. We have now extended the cross-species concordance analysis using the Cardoso-Moreira *et al.* (2019) developmental atlas of bulk RNA-seq across mammals (E-MTAB-6814). We applied the same pre-specified pipeline used for skeletal muscle: (i) restrict gene-level data to a strict one-to-one pig <-> human ortholog set (see R3.2 response); (ii) compute log2 fold-change between Infant (newborn/infant/toddler postnatal samples) and Adult (youngAdult/youngMidAge/olderMidAge/senior postnatal samples) in each species; (iii) test concordance via Pearson correlation with bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (1,000 iterations) on genes passing FDR < 0.10 (Benjamini-Hochberg) and |log2FC| > 0.5 in both species, with a pig-anchored alternative reported where the small Cardoso-Moreira *per-tissue* postnatal sample size renders per-gene human FDR underpowered.

One important caveat: the Cardoso-Moreira 2019 human RPKM matrix (`Human.RPKM.tsv` derived from `Human.RPKM.txt`, E-MTAB-6814) contains seven organs (Brain, Cerebellum, Heart, Kidney, Liver, Ovary, Testis) but **does not include lung**. We therefore performed the comparison for the two reviewer-requested tissues that *are* present in this dataset (Brain, Liver) and added Kidney as a third independent comparator so that the new panel (Supplementary Fig. S7) reports four tissues in total (Muscle [previously published, r = 0.69], Brain, Liver, Kidney). The lung omission is now stated explicitly in the limitations of the revised Results 2.4, and we discuss its implication: pig lung development can still be staged by our classifier (mean BA 0.892), but its cross-species concordance with humans cannot be assessed with the Cardoso-Moreira atlas alone.

Per-tissue results, sample sizes, top conserved genes, and overlap with the original 66-gene muscle set are tabulated in `review/analyses/results/cross_species_all_tissues.json` and visualised in Supplementary Fig. S7 (panels (a)-(c): per-tissue log2FC scatter plots with Pearson r; panel (d): forest plot of r ± 95% CI across all four tissues). The analysis script is `review/analyses/cross_species_all_tissues.py`; the validated ortholog table is `review/analyses/results/pig_human_one_to_one_orthologs.csv`; the long-format gene-level table is `review/analyses/results/cross_species_per_tissue_genes.csv`. All conclusions are framed in the conservative "directional concordance of infant-to-adult net change" language already used for muscle, given that Cardoso-Moreira contributes a small number of postnatal samples per tissue.
