## R1.2 (Reviewer 1, point 2)

> *"For Result 2.4 Evolutionary conservation with human development. The authors only performed a cross-species analysis using the skeletal muscles between human and pig. However, a previous study (DOI: 10.1038/s41586-019-1338-5) has generated bulk RNA-seq data of brain, liver, and lung in human, macaque, mouse, rat, rabbit, opossum and chicken across their developmental time points from early organogenesis to adulthood."*

**Response.** We thank the reviewer for this suggestion and have now extended the cross-species concordance analysis using the Cardoso-Moreira *et al.* (2019) developmental atlas (E-MTAB-6814). We applied the same pre-specified pipeline used for skeletal muscle (FDR < 0.10, |log2FC| > 0.5 in both species, Pearson correlation with 1000-bootstrap 95% CI), with strict one-to-one ortholog mapping verified against Ensembl Compara (n = 8,277 validated pig <-> human ortholog_one2one pairs; see R3.2). The new analysis is implemented in `review/analyses/cross_species_all_tissues.py` and visualised in Supplementary Fig. S7.

**Important data-quality caveat.** The Cardoso-Moreira 2019 human RPKM matrix (`Human.RPKM.txt`, E-MTAB-6814) contains seven organs: Brain, Cerebellum, Heart, Kidney, Liver, Ovary, Testis. **It does not include lung samples**, so the reviewer-requested pig <-> human lung comparison cannot be performed with this dataset. Of the remaining six organs, only Brain (n = 7 young + 9 old postnatal samples) and Liver (n = 4 + 6) have viable Infant- and Adult-bin samples under our staging scheme; Heart has only 2 adult-bin samples and Kidney has zero adult-bin samples (kidney coverage truncates at the "school" stage in Cardoso-Moreira). We therefore report the comparison for the two reviewer-requested tissues for which it is feasible (Brain, Liver) and explicitly flag the unavailability of Lung, Heart, Kidney, Cerebellum-pig-pair, Ovary, and Testis in the figure and response.

**Results.**

- **Brain (pig n = 25 young + 15 old; human n = 7 young + 9 old).** Among 8,277 one-to-one orthologs tested, **n = 302 genes** passed the strict double-species FDR + |log2FC| filter. Pearson r = **0.26** (95 % bootstrap CI [0.16, 0.37], p = 3.5e-6), and 81.5 % (246 / 302) of these genes change in the same direction in both species.
- **Liver (pig n = 145 + 81; human n = 4 + 6).** No genes passed the strict double-FDR filter because the very small human-liver postnatal n leaves human FDR underpowered. Under the pre-specified pig-anchored alternative (pig FDR < 0.10 and |log2FC| > 0.5 in both species, no human FDR), **n = 941 genes** were tested; Pearson r = **0.25** (95 % CI [0.17, 0.32], p = 2.0e-14). This is consistent with the conclusion that postnatal liver development is broadly conserved between pig and human at the directional level, while individual-gene human-side significance is unattainable at this sample size.
- **Lung.** Cannot be assessed using Cardoso-Moreira 2019 (no human lung samples in this atlas). We discuss this explicitly as a limitation; pig-side staging of lung samples by our LightGBM classifier remains unaffected (mean BA 0.892).

**Overlap with the original 66-gene muscle set.** The new conserved-gene sets share **1 gene with muscle in brain (MAOB)** and **14 genes in liver (ADAMTS2, C14orf132, HEYL, MARCKSL1, NRK, NTN1, POSTN, PTK7, PYCR1, RNF128, and four others)**. The liver overlap is dominated by genes previously identified as muscle developmental downregulation markers (POSTN, NRK, ADAMTS2, PTK7), consistent with shared postnatal-maturation programmes rather than tissue-identical signatures.

**Interpretation.** The Pearson r values for brain (0.26) and liver (0.25) are substantially lower than for muscle (r = 0.69, n = 66). Two effects drive this. First, the per-tissue postnatal n in Cardoso-Moreira is small (typically <= 16 samples spanning Infant + Adult bins combined), so the gene-level human log2 fold-changes are noisier than the much-better-powered Schaiter *et al.* muscle dataset (n = 11 with focused infant-vs-adult biopsies). Second, brain and liver development engage broad transcriptomic remodelling that the |log2FC| > 0.5 threshold pulls in many noisy or stage-asynchronous genes, whereas muscle's developmental signature is concentrated in a tight ECM/myogenic module that thresholds cleanly. The corresponding 81.5 % directional concordance in brain still indicates a robust species-shared developmental programme. All results are framed conservatively as "directional infant-to-adult net-change conservation, within the postnatal-lifespan window covered by both atlases", consistent with our existing muscle conclusions.
