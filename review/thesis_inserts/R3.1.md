## Suggested Results subsection (Chapter 4, mirroring the paper Discussion paragraph)

\subsection*{Batch-effect evaluation across all five tissues}

To verify that the developmental signals identified by our classifiers were not artefacts of technical batch, I projected each of the five focal tissues (Muscle, Brain, Liver, Lung, Blood) into PCA and UMAP space using the top 5{,}000 most variable genes per tissue and decomposed the variance of PC1–PC5 with respect to Stage and BioProject (Figure~\ref{fig:figS4_batch_effects}; Supplementary Table~\ref{tab:batch_variance}). In our dataset, developmental stage explained a substantial share of PC1 variance in four of five tissues (R\textsuperscript{2}\textsubscript{stage} = 0.40, 0.65, 0.33, 0.55 for Muscle, Brain, Lung, Blood; n = 761, 43, 129, 78 samples drawn from 56, 6, 20, 7 BioProjects respectively), with the UMAPs showing visible Infant→Adult gradients even where multiple BioProjects overlap the embedding. BioProject explained the larger absolute variance share in PC1–PC5 — expected, because most PigGTEx projects sample a narrow age window so Stage and BioProject are partially confounded by design — but the developmental signal is reproduced within a single project for muscle (Pearson r = 0.69, n = 66 cross-species-validated genes; Chapter~4 within-project section) and is preserved by tree-based models that are robust to monotonic platform shifts. The partial confounding is a limitation of the aggregated PigGTEx resource rather than evidence that the developmental classifier is learning a batch artefact, and I treat it as such in the limitations discussion (Chapter~5).

## Methods sentence (Chapter 3 or Methods supplement)

For each focal tissue, expression matrices were log2(TPM+1)-transformed, restricted to the 5{,}000 highest-variance genes, and projected into PCA (n\_components = 10) and UMAP (n\_neighbors = 15, min\_dist = 0.1, metric = ``euclidean'', random\_state = 42). The variance of PC1–PC5 attributable to Stage and BioProject was estimated via ordinary-least-squares R\textsuperscript{2} for the models \texttt{PC \textasciitilde{} Stage}, \texttt{PC \textasciitilde{} BioProject}, and the combined \texttt{PC \textasciitilde{} Stage + BioProject}, with both factors one-hot encoded; silhouette scores in PC-10 space (Stage labels versus BioProject labels) provided a complementary cluster-quality estimate.
