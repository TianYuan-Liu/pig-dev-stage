\paragraph{Extending the cross-species comparison beyond skeletal muscle.}
A natural follow-up to the muscle comparison is whether the directional
concordance we observe is muscle-specific or a more general property of
pig vs human postnatal development. To address this we re-applied the
exact pipeline used for muscle to additional non-muscle tissues using
the Cardoso-Moreira~\textit{et~al.}~(2019) developmental RNA-seq atlas
(ArrayExpress E-MTAB-6814)\cite{cardoso-moreira_gene_2019}, which
provides bulk RNA-seq across organogenesis to adulthood in seven
mammals.

\subparagraph{Tissue scope and data availability.}
The Cardoso-Moreira 2019 human RPKM matrix contains seven organs: brain,
cerebellum, heart, kidney, liver, ovary, and testis. \emph{Lung is not
included in this human dataset}, so the pig$\leftrightarrow$human lung
comparison the reviewer asked for cannot be performed with this atlas;
we discuss this directly here and in Supplementary Fig.~S7. Of the
remaining six organs, only brain (human $n=7$ Infant + $9$ Adult
postnatal samples) and liver ($n=4+6$) have viable Infant- and
Adult-bin sample counts under our staging scheme. Heart has only two
Adult-bin samples and kidney has zero (its coverage truncates at the
\textit{school} stage in this atlas), so neither can be assessed.

\subparagraph{Pipeline.}
Pig $\leftrightarrow$ human one-to-one orthologs were defined under a
strict criterion: candidate pairs were retained only if the underlying
gene symbol appears exactly once in each species' \texttt{mygene.info}
mapping (15{,}200 candidates), and each candidate was subsequently
verified against Ensembl Compara (REST homology endpoint), retaining
only pairs classified as \texttt{ortholog\_one2one} (8{,}277 validated
pairs; full Methods description in the Methods chapter). Log$_2$
fold-changes were computed between Infant (newborn/infant/toddler) and
Adult (youngAdult/youngMidAge/olderMidAge/senior) postnatal samples in
human, mirroring the Infant ($\le$~20\,d) vs Adult ($>$~365\,d)
contrast in pig. The gene-selection criteria from the muscle analysis
(FDR$<$0.10 Benjamini-Hochberg in both species, $|\log_2\mathrm{FC}|>0.5$
in both species) were applied identically. Where the small human
per-tissue $n$ leaves human FDR underpowered we additionally report a
pig-anchored result (pig FDR$<$0.10, $|\log_2\mathrm{FC}|>0.5$ in both
species, no human FDR filter) following the protocol from the
liver pilot analysis.

\subparagraph{Per-tissue concordance.}
For brain, $n=302$ of 8{,}277 one-to-one orthologs passed the strict
double-species filter, yielding Pearson $r=0.26$ (95\% bootstrap CI
0.16--0.37; $p=3.5\times 10^{-6}$) with 81.5\% (246/302) of genes
changing in the same direction in both species. For liver, no genes
passed the strict double-FDR filter because of the small human
per-tissue $n$ (4 vs 6 samples); under the pig-anchored alternative,
$n=941$ genes were tested with Pearson $r=0.25$ (95\% CI 0.17--0.32;
$p=2.0\times 10^{-14}$). Overlap between the new conserved-gene sets
and our original 66-gene muscle set is modest---one gene in brain
(\textit{MAOB}, an oxidative deamination enzyme upregulated in adult
tissues) and 14 genes in liver (notably \textit{POSTN},
\textit{ADAMTS2}, \textit{NRK}, \textit{PTK7}---all
developmental-downregulation markers also identified in muscle).
Together with the previously reported muscle correlation ($r=0.69$,
$n=66$, 95\% CI 0.54--0.80), the cross-tissue forest plot
(Supplementary Fig.~S7d) shows three tissue-wide confidence
intervals all strictly above zero (Muscle $\gg$ Brain $\sim$ Liver).

\subparagraph{Interpretation and caveats.}
We frame these new results conservatively. The brain and liver
correlation magnitudes ($r\sim 0.25$) are substantially lower than for
muscle ($r=0.69$): two factors contribute. First, the per-tissue
postnatal $n$ in Cardoso-Moreira (typically $\le$~15 samples
combined across Infant + Adult bins) means human log$_2$ fold-changes
are noisier than in the Schaiter~\textit{et~al.}\cite{schaiter_molecular_2024}
muscle data. Second, brain and liver postnatal development engage
broader transcriptomic remodelling than the tight ECM/myogenic
module that drives the muscle signature, so the
$|\log_2\mathrm{FC}|>0.5$ threshold pulls in more stage-asynchronous
genes. The 81.5\% directional concordance in brain is nonetheless a
strong indicator of a species-shared programme. As with muscle, all
conservation claims here refer to \emph{directional net infant-to-adult
change}; the sparse coverage of intermediate human postnatal stages
in Cardoso-Moreira does not allow a stage-by-stage trajectory
comparison. Finally, the pig$\leftrightarrow$human lung concordance
question remains open until a human postnatal lung developmental
cohort becomes available; pig-side staging of lung samples by our
LightGBM classifier (mean BA~$=$~0.89) is unaffected by this gap.
