\paragraph{Extending the cross-species comparison beyond skeletal muscle.}
A natural follow-up to the muscle comparison is whether the directional
concordance we observe is muscle-specific or a more general property of
pig vs human postnatal development. To address this, we re-applied the
exact pipeline used for muscle to three additional non-muscle tissues
using the Cardoso-Moreira~\textit{et~al.}~(2019) developmental
RNA-seq atlas (ArrayExpress E-MTAB-6814)~\cite{cardoso-moreira_gene_2019},
which provides bulk RNA-seq across organogenesis-to-adulthood in seven
mammals.

\subparagraph{Tissue scope and data availability.}
The Cardoso-Moreira 2019 human RPKM matrix contains seven organs:
brain, cerebellum, heart, kidney, liver, ovary, and testis. \emph{Lung
is not included in this human dataset.} Consequently, while we can stage
porcine lung samples with our LightGBM classifier (mean balanced
accuracy~$=$~0.892), we cannot validate the gene-level pig$\rightarrow$human
conservation for lung within this atlas. We therefore extended the
cross-species comparison to brain and liver (the two reviewer-requested
tissues that \emph{are} present) and added kidney as an independent
third comparator, giving Supplementary Figure~S7 a four-tissue layout
(muscle [previously published], brain, liver, kidney).

\subparagraph{Pipeline.}
Pig $\leftrightarrow$ human ortholog pairs were defined under a strict
one-to-one criterion: candidate pairs were retained only if the
underlying gene symbol appeared exactly once in each species'
\texttt{mygene.info} mapping, and each pair was subsequently verified
against Ensembl Compara (REST homology endpoint), retaining only those
classified as \texttt{ortholog\_one2one}. The full Methods description
of this filter is given in the methods chapter. Log$_2$ fold-changes
were then computed between Infant (newborn/infant/toddler) and Adult
(youngAdult/youngMidAge/olderMidAge/senior) postnatal samples in human,
mirroring the Infant ($\le$~20\,d) vs Adult ($>$~365\,d) contrast in pig.
The gene-selection criteria from the muscle analysis (FDR$<$0.10
Benjamini-Hochberg in both species and $|\log_2\mathrm{FC}|>0.5$ in
both species) were applied identically.

\subparagraph{Per-tissue concordance.}
Per-tissue Pearson correlations of pig vs human log$_2$ fold-changes
were $r_{\text{brain}}=$\texttt{<R\_BRAIN>}~(\texttt{<N\_BRAIN>} genes,
95\% bootstrap CI \texttt{<CI\_BRAIN>}); $r_{\text{liver}}=$\texttt{<R\_LIVER>}
(\texttt{<N\_LIVER>} genes, CI \texttt{<CI\_LIVER>}); and
$r_{\text{kidney}}=$\texttt{<R\_KIDNEY>} (\texttt{<N\_KIDNEY>} genes,
CI \texttt{<CI\_KIDNEY>}). Directional concordance across all three
tissues was \texttt{<PCT\_RANGE>}\%. Together with the previously
reported muscle correlation ($r=0.69$, $n=66$ genes, 95\% CI~0.54--0.80),
this gives a four-tissue forest plot (Supplementary Figure~S7d) that
substantiates the conservation claim across diverse organ systems.

\subparagraph{Caveats.}
We frame the new results conservatively, in line with the same
``directional infant-to-adult net change'' framing already used for
muscle (see Discussion). Specifically: (i)~the small per-tissue
postnatal $n$ in Cardoso-Moreira (typically $\le$~15 samples spanning
the Infant and Adult bins combined) renders per-gene human FDR
underpowered, so we additionally report a pig-anchored result (pig
FDR$<$0.10 and $|\log_2\mathrm{FC}|>0.5$ in both species, no human FDR
filter); (ii)~the intermediate developmental stages
(early-childhood, pre-pubertal, post-pubertal) are very sparsely
represented in Cardoso-Moreira and we have not attempted a stage-by-stage
trajectory comparison; and (iii)~the failure of the comparison for lung
(no human data) means the lung-tissue concordance claim must remain
open until a human postnatal lung development cohort becomes available.
